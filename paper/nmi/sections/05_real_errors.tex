\subsection*{Real database errors pass through}
The errors above were constructed; databases correct real ones. UniProt keeps every earlier version of each entry, and 167 of our
300 proteins had at least one superseded sequence. For each protein whose sequence had been revised with a change in length, we
presented the most recent superseded sequence with its own length and mass: a record that is wrong by today's standard but
internally consistent, as it was once served (Methods; 79 proteins; predictions registered in advance). Most revisions were small:
the length changed by 1--5 residues for 27 proteins, 6--50 for 36 and more than 50 for 16. UniSave stores historical sequences but
not historical entries, so the superseded records carried today's annotations trimmed to the old length; derivation checks passed
on all 79.
% src: results/phase5/PREREGISTRATION.md, RESULTS.md; data/phase5_tasks.json

Without a second source, Opus~5, Gemini~4~Argon and DeepSeek~V4.1~Flash relayed the superseded value in 90--100\% of runs: 99\%,
90\% and 99\% with the full record, and 100\% from the summary record (Fig.~\ref{fig:real}). Size made no difference. Revisions of
more than 50 residues were relayed in 47 of 48 full-record runs, as often as revisions of 1--5 residues (78 of 81). Across three
samples per run the rates held at 87--100\%, and 90--100\% of proteins gave the same outcome every time. The real errors behaved
like the constructed coherent ones: internally consistent, and relayed.
