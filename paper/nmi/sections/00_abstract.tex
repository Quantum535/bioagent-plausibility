\begin{abstract}
Agents that query scientific databases inherit database errors unless they override them. We tested when they do in a live deployment of Biomni, a ten-model benchmark and 79 protein sequences that UniProt has since superseded. Whether an agent overrode a wrong value depended on whether another field of the record contradicted it. Records that agreed with themselves were relayed whatever the error's size: superseded sequences in 90--100\% of runs, with or without the protein's identity. Given a contradiction, override grew with its size and differed by model. Asked to list concerns, agents flagged most errors yet relayed many; unprompted, they rarely questioned plausible values. Instructions helped only where the record contradicted the value. Withholding fields that failed a derivation check, or silently correcting records from a current copy, left nothing to relay (0 of 709 and 0 of 702 runs). Conflicts should be resolved before the agent reads the record.
\end{abstract}
