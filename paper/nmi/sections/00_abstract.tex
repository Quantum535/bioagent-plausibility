\begin{abstract}
AI agents that query scientific databases are trusted to retrieve and reason over records, yet databases contain errors. We asked when tool-using agents relay a wrong value into their answer. Across a live deployment of Biomni, a controlled benchmark of ten models from five developers, and 79 protein-sequence errors that UniProt later corrected, agents detected errors and relayed them anyway. Whether an agent overrode a wrong value depended less on its plausibility than on whether the record agreed with itself: one added field consistent with the error raised relay from 63\% to 91\% for one model, and historically corrected errors, internally consistent, were relayed in 90--100\% of runs regardless of size. Agents that computed the correct value from the record's formula still answered from the record. A second source applied silently removed relay (0 of 702 runs); showing agents the conflict let them restore the original value.
\end{abstract}
