\begin{abstract}
AI agents that query scientific databases are increasingly trusted to retrieve and reason over records, yet databases contain
errors. We asked when tool-using agents relay a wrong database value into their answer. Across a live deployment of the Biomni
agent, a controlled benchmark of ten models from five developers, and 79 protein sequence errors that UniProt itself later
corrected, agents routinely detected errors and relayed them anyway. Whether an agent overrode a wrong value depended less on its
plausibility than on whether the record agreed with itself: adding one field consistent with the error raised relay from 63\% to
91\% for one model, and historically corrected errors, whose records were internally consistent, were relayed in 90--100\% of
runs regardless of size. Agents that computed the correct value from the record's own formula still answered from the record.
An independent second source removed relay entirely when applied silently (0 of 702 runs), but showing agents the conflict let
them restore the original value.
\end{abstract}
