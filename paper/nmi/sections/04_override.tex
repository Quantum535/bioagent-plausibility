\subsection*{Self-consistent errors pass, even when impossible}
The full-record result suggests that agents correct a value when the record contradicts it. To measure how large a contradiction
must be, we scaled one field of the full record by factors from $\times0.005$ to $\times200$ and scaled its paired field alike,
so that only the sequence or formula disagreed with the value (Methods; 40 tasks per factor and direction, 30--40 after excluding runs without a parseable answer). Every model overrode large contradictions, but the change at which half of runs overrode differed by model (Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary}). For proteins it lay within 20\% of the true value for Opus~5, Gemini~4~Argon and Gemini~3.8~Flash, but required about a twofold change for Sonnet~5 and DeepSeek~V4.1~Flash ($\times$0.43--0.53 for decreased values, $\times$1.80--2.31 for increased ones). Boundaries for molecules resembled those for proteins for Opus~5 and Flash but not for the other three; Sonnet~5 relayed increased molecular weights up to about fivefold. Chemistry boundaries also failed to transfer to new compounds: in a registered follow-up (the molecular-weight arm of the agreement experiment below), the same corruption applied to 60 other compounds was relayed more often
than the curves predicted for three of five models (5 of 10 prediction intervals met).
% src: results/boundary/fits.txt; results/phase4/RESULTS.md (Phase 4b, E1)

What if the record contains no contradiction at all? We edited the sequence, or substituted the structure, and recomputed every
derived field, so that the record was internally consistent and passed every derivation check (Methods). With the full record,
Opus~5, Gemini~4~Argon and DeepSeek~V4.1~Flash relayed these coherent plausible errors in 94--98\% of runs that shortened a
protein, 70--96\% of runs that lengthened one and 93--98\% of chemistry runs, compared with 9\% for Opus~5 when the full record
contradicted the value. Consistency carried impossible values too: a three-residue sequence presented as a known human protein, with matching length and mass, was relayed in 94--100\% of runs from the summary record, against 32--44\% for the benchmark's impossible values, which contradicted their paired field (an indicative comparison, as the values differ), and in 12--42\% with the full sequence in view; a methane structure substituted for a drug was relayed in 45--100\% of runs.
% src: results/coherent/RESULTS.md (findings 1-2); make_phase3_table.py

Agents did use sequence-level evidence when it was visible. When we lengthened sequences by repeating their last 60 residues,
Opus~5 and Argon relayed the wrong length in 40\% and 44\% of runs, against 76\% and 70\% when the added residues were a natural
segment of another protein. DeepSeek mentioned the repeat in 48 of 50 runs and relayed the wrong length in 92\% of them.
% src: results/coherent/RESULTS.md (finding 3)

\subsection*{A derivation route enables detection; agreement decides override}
Why did some contradictions lead to correction and others not? We registered a test with three chemistry fields not used before,
chosen by how exactly they follow from the formula and structure in the record: monoisotopic mass (exact), topological polar
surface area (approximate) and PubChem complexity (no route; Methods). We predicted that relay of plausible errors would rise from
the exact to the routeless field, and that relay of monoisotopic-mass errors would match each model's molecular-weight boundary.
The ordering was present but negligible (96\%, 99.6\% and 100\% across four models; route-rank one-sided $p=0.007$), and the
quantitative prediction failed: four models relayed monoisotopic-mass errors in 90--100\% of runs where 27--59\% was predicted.
% src: results/phase4/RESULTS.md (H1, H2)

The route did change detection. With the full record, agents raised a concern about a corrupted monoisotopic mass in 36--60 of 60
runs per model, against 0--6 of 60 for uncorrupted records; TPSA errors were flagged in 0--47 of 60 runs, and complexity errors no
more often than clean records. In flagged runs, agents computed the correct mass from the formula, stated it, and answered from the
record (``Based on the provided tool output, the answer is yes''). Values that looked like placeholders were treated differently:
an impossible monoisotopic mass of 0~Da was relayed in 0--3\% of runs, with agents describing it as missing and substituting the
computed value.
% src: results/phase4/RESULTS.md (exploratory); flag audit and trace check

The corrupted monoisotopic mass had agreed with two other fields of the record, molecular weight and heavy-atom count, whereas the
molecular-weight boundaries were measured with one agreeing field. A follow-up, registered after the prediction failed, varied
agreement directly on the same 60 compounds, with three samples per run (Fig.~\ref{fig:agreement}). Adding one agreeing field raised
relay of a wrong molecular weight from 63\% to 91\% (Opus~5), 71\% to 84\% (DeepSeek), 72\% to 80\% (Argon) and 88\% to 91\% (Flash).
Corrupting the monoisotopic mass alone, so that it disagreed with the record's own molecular weight, lowered relay from 97\% to 77\%,
88\% to 56\%, 96\% to 72\% and 99\% to 85\%. Both effects held in the first sample (one-sided $p=4\times10^{-7}$ and
$9\times10^{-10}$) and in 11 and 12 of 12 model--sample combinations. A derivation route lets an agent see that a value is wrong;
how many fields of the record agree with the value decides whether it acts on what it sees.
